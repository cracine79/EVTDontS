This is Navin. He works in a gas station, and he earns a whopping $500 a week. Navin comes up with an invention, the Opti-Grab, which reduces wear on the earpieces of eyeglasses, and becomes a millionaire overnight. Let's make a modest assumption that Navin's income increases to $5,500 a week. This would represent a 1,000% increase in Navin's income. As a result of this income increase, let's say that he increases his purchases of new cars in a year from one new car to six new cars, or an increase of 500% in his quantity demanded of new cars. We can measure how sensitive Navin's demand for automobiles is to changes in income using the concept of income elasticity of demand.

Income elasticity of demand is calculated as the percentage change in quantity demanded of a good divided by the percentage change in income. Remember that income is the money that households earn from providing their factors of production to the resource market. This includes wages for providing labor, rental income from providing capital and land, interest income from providing financial capital, as well as profits from entrepreneurial activity. So this thing right here, the income elasticity of demand coefficient, is telling us: if the incomes of households go up by a certain percentage, by how much does quantity demanded for this particular good change? In the case of automobiles, we would measure Navin's income elasticity of demand as 500%, the increase in his quantity demanded for automobiles, divided by 1,000%, the increase in income which caused him to buy more automobiles. This gives us positive 0.5.

Notice that when Navin earned more income, he bought more automobiles. Because of this phenomenon, income elasticity of demand for automobiles is positive. As such, we classify automobiles as normal goods. Normal goods are defined as goods that people will buy more of when incomes increase.

His income plummets, and he buys fewer automobiles. For normal goods, when incomes decrease, quantity demanded decreases. Income elasticity of demand is positive for normal goods because when incomes increase (a positive percentage change in income), this causes an increase in quantity demanded for normal goods (a positive change). A positive divided by a positive is a positive. When incomes decrease (a negative percentage change), this causes quantity demanded for normal goods to decrease (a negative percentage change). A negative divided by a negative is a positive. Income elasticity of demand is positive for normal goods.

Necessity goods are normal goods, in that when incomes rise, people buy more of them, but the increase in quantity demanded for necessity goods is smaller, relatively speaking, than the increase in income. Navin's income increases 10 times, but he only buys twice as much toothpaste. If an increase in income results in a smaller increase in quantity demanded, then this fraction right here is always going to be less than one, as the bottom is going to be bigger than the top. As such, necessity goods are goods that have an income elasticity of demand less than one, where incomes increase and quantity demanded for those goods increases by a smaller percentage. A luxury good is a good that sees a larger increase in quantity demanded driven by an increase in income. As a result, luxury goods will have an income elasticity of demand coefficient greater than one: if an increase in income yields a bigger increase in quantity demanded, then this number is going to be greater than one.

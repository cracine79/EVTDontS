For substitute goods, an increase in the price of good X increases the demand for good Y, and a decrease in the price of good X decreases the demand for good Y.

Complement goods are the other type of related goods. Complement goods are goods that are often consumed in conjunction: cars and gasoline, iPads and iPad apps, peanut butter and jelly. If the price of peanut butter goes up, quantity demanded for peanut butter falls (the law of demand), so demand for jelly also falls. If the price of peanut butter goes down, people buy more peanut butter, so people also buy more jelly, and the demand for jelly increases.
